# Silver Blaze: evidence in an absence

Canonical fiction, summarized for this pack. In Silver Blaze, Holmes draws Inspector Gregory's attention to a dog that did not bark during the relevant night. The silence is significant because a familiar person could approach without alarming the dog. The general lesson is that an absence matters only when there is reason to expect something else. Silence alone does not prove who acted, and the episode does not authorize inventing missing evidence in a new puzzle.
Source: Arthur Conan Doyle, [SILV: Silver Blaze (1892), conversation with Inspector Gregory](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Memoirs_of_Sherlock_Holmes_1894_Burt/Silver_Blaze).
